1. Introduction & Project Overview

Motivation, Clinical Fragmentation & Proposed Decentralized Ecosystem

Overview

Healthcare Data Vulnerabilities

  • Centralized Single Point of Failure: Healthcare databases are prime targets for ransomware, insider data tampering, and accidental outages.
  • Clinical Data Fragmentation: Patients consult disparate hospitals, diagnostic labs, and clinics, leaving diagnostic histories scattered.
  • Redundant Diagnostic Scans: Inability to locate previous test results forces doctors to repeat costly CT scans, MRIs, and blood tests.
  • Storage Bloat: Identical reports uploaded across visits consume terabytes of un-deduplicated server storage.

Proposed Blockchain Framework

  • Hybrid Off-Chain / On-Chain Architecture: Heavy medical reports are AES-256 encrypted off-chain; only cryptographic SHA-256 digests and storage proofs reside on-chain.
  • Strict Cryptographic Deduplication: In-memory SHA-256 hash checking against the ledger blocks duplicate uploads before storage.
  • Longitudinal Timeline: Synthesizes reports across multi-institution visits into a single chronological view.
  • Instant Anti-Forgery Verification: Dynamic QR codes verify document authenticity directly against immutable ledger blocks.
Core Motivation: Balancing clinical accessibility, patient confidentiality, data immutability, and zero storage bloat.

3. Limitations of Existing Systems

Synthesis of the 13 Critical Technical Bottlenecks Identified in Literature

Research Gaps

1. High Cryptographic Complexity

Complex proxy re-encryption (PRE) and attribute-based encryption (ABE) introduce immense configuration hurdles.

2. Heavy Storage Overhead

Storing multi-megabyte diagnostic PDFs, X-rays, and MRI scans on-chain causes rapid ledger bloat and node failure.

3. Difficulty in Access Revocation

Traditional systems grant permanent access; revoking rights from consulting doctors requires complex key rebuilding.

4. Public Ledger Privacy Violations

Publishing raw patient health data onto public blockchains violates HIPAA and GDPR "Right to be Forgotten" mandates.

5. Redundant Diagnostic Testing

Lack of cross-facility medical timelines forces patients to repeatedly undergo traumatic, costly duplicate scans.

6. Absence of Duplicate Detection

Current EHRs fail to cryptographically fingerprint files, resulting in multiple unverified copies of identical reports.

Key Takeaway: Existing systems either over-complicate cryptography or under-deliver on real-world medical usability.

4. Problem Statement & Objectives

Clear Clinical Rationale & The Three Specific, Measurable Project Objectives

Objectives

Problem Statement

Existing healthcare systems primarily rely on centralized databases and conventional methods for storing and managing medical records. Such systems face challenges related to data security, unauthorized access, data integrity, privacy, and controlled sharing. Managing and distributing sensitive medical reports through conventional centralized systems makes it difficult to ensure that only authorized users can access them and that report integrity can be verified without storing heavy files on-chain.

Objective 1

Reduce Repeated Diagnostic Tests

Enable healthcare professionals to retrieve and review a patient's previously recorded diagnostic reports across healthcare encounters, eliminating repeated tests.

Objective 2

Detect Duplicate Reports via SHA-256

Compute unique cryptographic SHA-256 fingerprints upon upload to verify uniqueness against existing ledger blocks and maintain a single verified reference.

Objective 3

Chronological Medical History

Organize a patient's medical history by generating a consolidated chronological timeline of reports that gives clinicians a longitudinal view of clinical progression.

Role-Based Access Control, Cryptographic Ingestion & Anti-Forgery Services

Requirements 1/2

🔐 User Authentication & RBAC

  • Lab Staff: Exclusively authorized to upload diagnostic reports & trigger blockchain mining.
  • Doctor: Permitted to search, review historical records, and append clinical notes.
  • Patient: Restricted strictly to personal medical history; cannot alter reports.
  • Admin: Manages consortium nodes and system configuration.

⚡ Upload & Duplicate Detection

  • Streaming Ingestion: Multer middleware parses multipart PDF/image diagnostic reports in memory.
  • In-Memory SHA-256 Digest: Generates unique 256-bit hash fingerprint before storage.
  • Ledger Pre-Check: Queries existing blockchain blocks; rejects exact duplicates with HTTP 409 Conflict.
  • Off-Chain AES Encryption: Files encrypted with AES-256-CBC prior to disk persistence.

🔍 Retrieval & Anti-Forgery

  • QR Code Anti-Forgery: Unique QR encodes report verification URI pointing to anchored blockchain hash.
  • Tamper Detection: Scanned file bytes are re-hashed; returns live MATCH or MISMATCH.
  • Time-Expiring Sharing: Signed JWTs allow patients to grant temporary access with auto-expiration.
  • Smart OCR Tagging: Tesseract.js extracts key terms (e.g. "Blood Sugar") for fast tag searching.

6. System Design: Algorithms & Cryptographic Models

SHA-256 Deduplication, AES-256-CBC Pipeline & Blockchain Integrity Formulae

Design 3/5
ALGORITHM 1

Cryptographic Deduplication

Given report byte buffer \( B \):
1. \( H(B) = \text{SHA-256}(B) \)
2. Query ledger for any block \( K \) where \( K.\text{data.reportHash} == H(B) \)
3. If match found: Abort and return 409 CONFLICT.
4. If unique: Proceed to AES encryption.

Time Complexity: O(n) ledger scan / O(1) indexed
ALGORITHM 2

AES-256-CBC Off-Chain Encryption

1. Generate cryptographically secure random 16-byte \( IV \).
2. Initialize cipher \( C = \text{Cipher}(\text{AES-256-CBC}, K_{\text{master}}, IV) \).
3. Encrypt payload: \( E = C.\text{update}(B) \parallel C.\text{final}() \).
4. Serialize binary: \( \text{Payload} = IV \parallel E \).

Security: Indistinguishable under CPA
ALGORITHM 3

Block Chaining & Hash Validation

Block hash calculation:
\( H_{\text{block}} = \text{SHA-256}(\text{idx} \parallel H_{\text{prev}} \parallel T \parallel \text{Data} \parallel \text{nonce}) \)
Tamper Detection condition:
\( \forall i > 0, \text{Block}[i].H_{\text{prev}} == \text{Block}[i-1].H_{\text{block}} \)

Guarantee: Cryptographically Tamper-Evident
Mathematical Foundation: 256-bit collision resistance ensures zero duplicate hash collisions across \( 2^{128} \) uploads.
